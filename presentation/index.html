An evolution simulation · kin-dots

Dots that give
their lives

Simple creatures, no rule about family, and yet most of them end up dying for a neighbour.

The puzzle

Evolution keeps whatever gets copied.

So a gene that makes its carrier die should disappear.

Here is a world where it spreads instead.

The world

A square of grass with no edges

Walk off the right side and you come back on the left. The same goes for top and bottom. Glue both pairs of edges and the map becomes the surface of a donut: a torus.

300²units of space
75²grass cells
2,500dots at start

flat map

The rules

Four rules everyone follows

Seasons

Grass regrows fast in summer and almost not at all in midwinter.

1 year = 600 steps

Eat & move

Dots drift slowly towards grass and eat it. Every step of life costs energy.

0.1 units per step

Die

Of hunger at zero energy, or of old age.

max age 300 steps

Split

At 20 energy a dot splits in two. The child appears next to it, with slightly mutated genes.

asexual · no mates
One extra option

Give everything away, and die

Each step a dot meets a random neighbour. It may hand over all its energy and die. The neighbour receives 90% of it.

p = σ( a + b · E / 20 )
aoverall urge to give everything away
bdoes the urge rise or fall with own energy E?
starta = −9, b = 0 → about 1 in 8,000 per step

The experiment

Two worlds, one difference

left world

Gift to a neighbour

The energy goes to the dot it just met. Children are born next to their parents and barely move, so that neighbour is usually family.

right world · control

Gift to a stranger

The energy goes to a random dot anywhere on the map. Same rules, same random seed.

A dot that sacrifices itself has no more children. After 30,000 steps, will there be

more sacrifice, or less?

Why it happens
r · B > C
r

relatedness

Giver and recipient share genes. Measured between neighbours: about 0.75.

B

benefit

Offspring the recipient gains: several units of energy, a big step towards splitting or surviving winter.

C

cost

Offspring the giver loses. Small for a weak dot in winter, which is why the weak give.

gift to a neighbourr ≈ 0.75 · rB > C
gift to a strangerr ≈ 0 · only the cost is left

Fine print

What this model does not show

Clones, not siblings

Reproduction is asexual, so relatives are almost clones. With sex, relatedness is lower and the effect would be weaker.

A world tuned for clarity

Milder winters or faster dots weaken it to 40–60% of deaths, still well above the control. The control matters more than the number.

A risky start

Before sacrifice spreads, early winters can shrink the population to a few dozen. An unlucky seed can die out.

B and C, not measured

Relatedness is measured directly. Benefit and cost in offspring are described, not computed.

Take-away

Selection acts on genes. Altruism is a by-product.
