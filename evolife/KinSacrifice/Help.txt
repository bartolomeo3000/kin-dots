

Kin sacrifice
=============

Dots may give ALL their energy to a neighbour and die.
Nothing in the rules tells them to help relatives, and they cannot recognise relatives.
Yet self-sacrifice spreads, until most deaths are voluntary.
It does so only when the gift goes to a neighbour (usually a relative),
not when it goes to a random stranger.

Rules (both worlds)
-------------------
- Grass grows on a torus. It regrows much more slowly in winter (one year = SeasonLength steps).
- Dots walk slowly towards grass and eat it. Every step costs energy.
- A dot dies of hunger (energy 0) or of old age (MaxAge steps).
- With BirthThreshold energy, a dot splits in two: the child appears next to it and inherits
  its genes with small mutations (asexual reproduction).
- At each step a dot meets a random neighbour (within MeetRadius) and, with probability
      p = sigmoid(a + b * energy / BirthThreshold)
  gives it all its energy (the recipient gets GiftEfficiency of it) and dies.
  a and b are genes. At start a = -9: sacrifice is very rare.
  A third gene, how strongly a dot heads towards grass, also evolves (similarly in both worlds).

The only difference
-------------------
Left:  the energy goes to the neighbour that was met. Since children are born next to their
       parents and dots barely move, neighbours are usually close relatives.
Right: (control) the energy goes to a random dot anywhere in the world: a stranger.

What to watch
-------------
- Curves (red = left world, blue = right world):
  thick: % of deaths over the last year that are self-sacrifice
  thin:  population size, in hundreds
  On the left, sacrifice rises from a few % to about 70% of all deaths (after ~10,000-20,000 steps).
  On the right, it stays low.
- The rule that evolves is "sacrifice yourself when you are weak" (b becomes negative):
  weak dots are unlikely to survive the winter anyway, and their energy goes to relatives
  carrying the same genes. This is Hamilton's rule (r B > C) at work: the right world is
  identical except that r is about 0.
- The Field window (key F) shows both worlds: grass in green, dots coloured by
     1 = urge to sacrifice (blue = never, yellow = often)
     2 = family (inherited colour, mutates slowly: patches of one colour are families)
     3 = energy
  White lines are the gifts of the last step (short on the left, long on the right).
  Above each world: the population now, and its mean / min / max over the last year.
  Key 0 shows / hides the grass. Keys 0, 1, 2, 3 work in the main window.
- The 'Families (hue)' window (key T) follows the inherited family colours of both worlds:
     top:    how many dots carry each hue right now
     bottom: the same over the whole run, time running downward; a vertical band is a family.
  On the left many families persist side by side; on the right, winter crashes keep wiping
  most of them out and a few survivors take over.

Notes
-----
- The early winters are a bottleneck: before sacrifice spreads, the population may drop to
  a few dozen dots. With an unlucky seed a world may die out; restart with another seed.
- Port of the 'kin-dots' project (NumPy). SacrificeWorld.py can also run without graphics:
      python SacrificeWorld.py <steps> <seed> <winter harshness>
